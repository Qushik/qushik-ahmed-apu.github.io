Key design decision · A 28 m clarifier satisfied hydraulic loading but failed the solids loading rate (SLR) check, so the secondary clarifier was redesigned to 38 m diameter, achieving SLR = 5.0 kg/m²·h and reducing the risk of sludge blanket rise and biomass washout.

Nitrogen Removal Modification

  • Converted the conventional activated sludge train to a pre-anoxic system: Primary Effluent → Anoxic Tank → Aerobic/Nitrification Tank → Secondary Clarifier
  • Internal mixed liquor recycle (aerobic → anoxic) and RAS (clarifier → anoxic) drive nitrification–denitrification without an external carbon source

Sludge Management

  • Waste activated sludge thickened, then stabilised via anaerobic digestion
  • Biogas collected, cleaned and used for energy recovery; remaining digestate stabilised for disposal/reuse

R2 additionally achieved the best ammonia-nitrogen removal (52.2%) and the best chlorine residual retention (41.8% at 180 minutes), against 5.6% for R3 over the same period.
Key finding · R2 (mid-range 10-day SRT) delivered the strongest overall nutrient removal and microbial viability, while the longest-SRT reactor (R1) paradoxically achieved the weakest phosphorus removal — evidence that SRT alone does not determine treatment performance; SRT, feeding frequency and reactor hydrodynamics interact together.
